6. **The real SEO win is engagement + PR + a new keyword cluster**, not stuffing "virtual try-on" onto money pages. See §5 and §6.

## 2. ⭐ Will this hurt my current rankings? (Your main question — full answer)

**Short answer: No — if we add, never rewrite.** Here is the reasoning and the rules.

### Why cannibalization happens (and how we avoid it)
Keyword cannibalization is when **two of your own pages compete for the *same* keyword**, so Google can't decide which to rank, and both drop. It happens when people *rewrite an existing page's title/H1/focus keyword* to chase a new term.

Virtual try-on is a **completely different keyword cluster** from your money keywords:

| Existing cluster (DO NOT TOUCH) | New cluster (NEW page only) |
|---|---|
| ladies formal dresses Pakistan | virtual try-on dress Pakistan |
| Pakistani clothing brand | AI try on clothes online Pakistan |
| small clothing brand | try dress before buying online |
| Pakistani fashion online | virtual try room / virtual fitting room |

Because these clusters **don't overlap**, a new page targeting the right column **cannot compete with** your pages targeting the left column. Different intent, different URLs, different keywords = no cannibalization.

### The 5 non-negotiable rules that protect current SEO
1. **Do NOT change the `<title>`, meta description, H1, or focus keyword of any existing page** — homepage, `/about/`, `/ladies/`, product pages. Their current keywords stay exactly as they are.
2. **All virtual-try-on *ranking* lives on ONE new URL** (`/virtual-try-room/`). New page, new keyword target — additive to the site, not a replacement.
3. **On existing pages, virtual try-on appears only as a *supporting trust signal* — never as the page's primary keyword.** Example: a small homepage band that links out to `/virtual-try-room/`. The homepage still targets "Pakistani fashion online"; the band just adds a feature highlight. This *helps* (engagement, internal links) and competes with nothing.
4. **One canonical home for the topic.** The dedicated page is the canonical authority for "virtual try-on". Blog posts and the homepage band *link to it*, they don't try to out-rank it.
5. **No keyword stuffing on money pages.** Don't sprinkle "virtual try-on" into the ladies/about title tags hoping to rank — that's exactly what *would* dilute them.

### Net effect on existing rankings
- **Money pages:** unchanged keywords → **rankings unaffected.**
- **Bonus upside:** product pages gain dwell time (people use the try-on), and a new internal-link hub passes authority around — both are *positive* ranking signals. So the realistic outcome is **neutral-to-slightly-positive** for existing terms, plus a **brand-new** stream of traffic from the try-on cluster.

**Bottom line:** Adding a separate page + a self-contained homepage band + a blog pillar is *additive growth*. The thing that would hurt you — rewriting your proven pages — is exactly what this plan forbids.

### 4.6 Journal / blog — the keyword pillar (ongoing, but planned)
This is where you capture the long-tail and feed AI answer engines. Each post links back to `/virtual-try-room/`. Suggested first posts (these are *new* URLs — additive, no cannibalization):

3. "AI Try-On for Eid & Wedding Suits: See Yourself Before You Order"

> Note: blog post #2 directly targets *"Pakistani brands with virtual try-on"*. Writing the comparison yourself is how you get cited when someone asks an AI/Google to "recommend Pakistani brands with virtual try-on."

### 4.7 Homepage announcement pop-up (Virtual Try Room) — SAFE PATTERN
A scroll-triggered modal announcing the Virtual Try Room. **Modeled on a real, SEO-safe competitor implementation** (farinaofficial.com — brand "Farina"), with upgrades.

**Competitor reference (Farina) — what they do RIGHT (verified June 2026):**
- Fires **on scroll**, not on page load → visitor is already engaged, so it is NOT an "entry interstitial" (the thing Google penalizes).
- Shows **once per visitor** (persists across reloads via cookie/localStorage).
- **Dismissible**, with site content dimmed/visible behind it (not a hard takeover).
- Content = "UNSURE ABOUT YOUR FIT? Our virtual try-on has you covered" + a before/after polaroid + 3 steps. *Weakness: it's a dead-end explainer with no CTA/link.*

**Why this is SEO-safe:** Google's *intrusive interstitial* penalty targets pop-ups that block content **immediately on arrival from search**, mainly on **mobile**. A scroll/delay-triggered, dismissible, once-per-visitor overlay does **not** trigger it. It also doesn't touch any title/H1/keyword, so **zero cannibalization** (per §2).

**Recommended spec for Habiba Minhas:**
| Setting | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | On scroll past the hero (~70% of viewport) **OR** 5s delay | Engaged user → no entry-interstitial penalty; appears early, not far down the page |
| Frequency | Once per page view, on every homepage load/reload (no saved flag) | A visitor who closes it by accident gets another chance next reload |
| Dismiss | ✕ button **+** click-outside **+** ESC | Easy exit = not "intrusive" |
| Mobile | Card that does NOT fill the viewport; instantly closable | Interstitial penalty is mobile-specific — care here |
| Loading | Lazy / client-side; modal assets load AFTER main content | Protects LCP / Core Web Vitals |
| **CTA** | **Button → `/virtual-try-room/`** (or a try-on-enabled product) | ⭐ Turns the pop-up into SEO value, not just a click |

**Upgrade over Farina:** add the CTA button (Farina has none) so the pop-up funnels traffic into the cornerstone hub + first-mover story. Keep their before/after polaroid visual — the transformation is what convinces.

**Do NOT:** fire it instantly on load · make it full-screen on mobile · re-show every page/visit · hide the close button · let its images block initial render. Any one of these flips it from safe to penalty-risk.

## 6. Off-page — where the real ranking lift comes from

On-page gets you eligible; these get you *ranked* and *known*.

1. **PR / digital coverage** — "Pakistani brand launches AI virtual try-on" is a free-press headline. Pitch 5–10 outlets/bloggers (Dawn Images, ProPakistani, TechJuice, fashion IG pages). Each pickup = a **backlink** to `/virtual-try-room/` (the single biggest ranking lever).
2. **Google Business Profile** — post about the feature; helps local + brand SERP.
3. **Social proof loop** — encourage users to share their try-on result (watermarked) → UGC + branded searches → "Habiba Minhas virtual try-on" becomes a real query you own.
4. **YouTube/Reel demo** — a 30-sec "watch me try this on with AI" video, embedded on `/virtual-try-room/`. Video boosts dwell time and ranks in its own right (ties into your SEO roadmap's video goal).
5. **Internal links** — every blog post + the homepage band + footer point to the hub. Cheap, fully in your control, compounding.

## 7. Governance — "build once, don't churn" (your no-frequent-changes rule)

You're right that constant content changes hurt SEO. The discipline:

- **Lock the URL `/virtual-try-room/` forever.** Never rename/move it. If you ever must, set up a 301 redirect (don't just delete).
- **Lock the title tag + H1** of the hub page after launch. Tweak body copy occasionally; leave the title/H1/URL alone.
- **Never retrofit "virtual try-on" into existing money-page titles** to "boost" them — that's the one move that *would* hurt current rankings.
- **Add content as new URLs** (blog posts), not by rewriting old ones. New pages = growth; rewriting proven pages = risk.
- **Set-and-forget surfaces:** footer link, About paragraph, homepage band — write them once, well, then leave them.

> Healthy pattern: *new pages added over time, existing winners left untouched.* That's the opposite of churn.
